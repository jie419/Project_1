# Project_1
Project 1: Make An Ugly Page Cute

Live site: https://jie419.github.io/Project_1/

## Aesthetic: 

Cassette Futurism — https://cari.institute/aesthetics/cassette-futurism

## Reflection

**1. What was your main design strategy for organizing the information?**

I believe an attractive cover photo influences how willing I am to try a recipe, so I kept the title and description at the top, with an eye-catching photo right below. As someone who cooks often, what I care about most are the ingredients and instructions, since their complexity tells me how difficult the recipe is and how much effort it will take. That's why I placed the ingredients on the left side and gave it a dark background to make it stand out. When a user opens this site on their phone in a supermarket, they can clearly spot the uppercase "INGREDIENTS" label and quickly find the information they need. As for the aesthetic, Cassette Futurism reminded me of one of my favorite cafés in Taiwan, so I chose it and kept the palette in cool tones to match.

**2. How did you prioritize different pieces of content?**

I split the page into four main sections. For me, the ingredient list is the most important piece of information, since it tells me whether a recipe is trustworthy. I noticed Professor Alex treated prep time, cook time, and similar details as important information, but in my own experience, estimated times are usually not very reliable. So I kept that section relatively small, but not too small, so anyone who does want that information can still find it easily. I then made the instructions label stand out clearly, listing each step with bold numbering for quick scanning.

**3. What challenges did you encounter with responsive layout?**

Since I used border-left to separate Prep Time from Cook Time, it looked great on wide screens. But as I dragged the browser narrower, there was a range where the label text and the border would start to squeeze together. I solved this by adding more responsive breakpoints and reducing the padding, so the text's spacing would no longer exceed the width of the box.

**4. What trade-offs did your aesthetic require?**

The dark background on the ingredients panel and the overall cool, technical color palette look striking indoors, but they lose visibility in bright sunlight, which could make the page harder to read outdoors, for example on a phone in a sunny kitchen or grocery store. Similarly, the Chakra Petch and IBM Plex Mono fonts give the page a strong tech and futuristic feel, but stylized or monospace fonts like these can be harder for some users to read and process compared to more standard typefaces, particularly for people with dyslexia or other reading differences. So the Cassette Futurism aesthetic trades some accessibility and readability for visual identity and mood.
